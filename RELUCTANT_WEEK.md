# Reluctant-learner week: stop the daily battle

A free reset week from [Homeschool IRL](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/store/homeschool-irl).

This is for the child who melts down, negotiates, disappears, or does ten minutes and is done. You do not need a new curriculum. You need a smaller day.

Paid tools stay on TPT. This page is the week you can run from a notebook and a timer.

## What resistance is usually saying

| Pattern | Often means | This week's move |
|---|---|---|
| This is stupid | Work feels pointless or babyish | Real job with a real user |
| Stall, stall, stall | Start cost is too high | 20-minute sprint + two choices |
| Tears after ten minutes | Load is too big or skill is stuck | Cut volume, keep one skill |
| Fine until you sit down with a book | Reading is the roadblock | Read-aloud / audio / you scribe |
| Fine until math | Number sense or last year's hole | One problem type, kitchen math |
| Fine until you walk in the room | Control / audience problem | Independent work they chose |

If safety, hunger, sleep, or a learning difference is in the room, deal with that first.

## Rules for this week only

1. School is **20 focused minutes + one real-life job**. That is a complete day if that is all you get.
2. You offer **two choices**, both acceptable to you. Not "do you want to do school."
3. You do not pile on a missed subject at 8 p.m.
4. You name the win out loud.
5. No new boxed set.

## Daily shape

| Block | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Soft start | 5 min | Same seat, same first sentence |
| Sprint | 20 min | Timer visible. Stop when it rings. |
| Real job | 20-45 min | Kitchen, walk, repair, pet, mail |
| Optional second sprint | 20 min | Only if the first one did not blow up |
| Close | 2 min | What did you finish? One line |

If the sprint collapses, you still do the real job. The job is school.

## The week

**Monday — restart.** Choices: read with you for 20 minutes (they pick the book), or you read and they draw. Real job: dishwasher or feed an animal, then retell the sequence. Do not reopen last week's unfinished packet.

**Tuesday — useful to someone else.** Write a 5-sentence instruction or a 60-second voice note. Real job: measure a snack. [Life Skills Are Curriculum](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Life-Skills-Are-Curriculum-Real-Life-Learning-Activities-for-Homeschoolers-17557006).

**Wednesday — body first.** Walk with one question to look up later, or sketch three things at the window. Look it up after you are home: [NASA Climate Kids](https://climatekids.nasa.gov/).

**Thursday — a thing that has to work.** Fix one small thing, or make a list a real person will use. Test it. Change one thing. [The One-Activity Homeschool](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/The-One-Activity-Homeschool-Turn-One-Activity-into-Multiple-Subjects-17556974).

**Friday — evidence, then stop.** Show the best thing in 10 minutes, or you interview them. Write: what finished, where the fight lives, what next week inherits.

## Scripts

Start cost is high: "Two choices. Book with me, or you read and I write what you say. Timer starts when you pick."

They want to quit at minute four: "We stop at twenty. Four is not twenty. Sit. I will sit too."

They want a deal: "The deal is the timer. When it rings we are done with that block."

They say it is babyish: "Then pick the job a grown person in this house also has to do."

You are about to lecture: close the notebook. Do the real job. Talk later.

## Do not

Do not build a 14-column behavior chart. Do not withhold meals, outdoors, or sleep. Do not buy a new curriculum on Wednesday. Do not put paid TPT files in a co-op folder.

## After this week

Keep the small day. Add one subject bite, not five.
